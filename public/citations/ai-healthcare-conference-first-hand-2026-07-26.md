# AI in Healthcare Conference first-hand account

Recorded from conference chair Omar Choudhry on 26 July 2026. These statements are first-hand case-study evidence rather than independent evaluation.

## Panel

The panel brought together current PhD students from the UKRI Centre for Doctoral Training in AI for Medical Diagnosis and Care. Their work covered AI in medicine and surgery, and their academic, professional and industry routes into the field differed.

The shared theme was how people enter healthcare AI research despite different starting points. The panel aimed to discuss the benefits and realities of undertaking a PhD while remaining useful to people from the wider community who were considering research or trying to understand the field.

## Digital pathology workshop

The NPIC session was a digital pathology workshop led by three former graduates of the same CDT. Their doctoral work involved digital histopathology. They subsequently moved into work with a Leeds company that maintains strong relationships with the University of Leeds and the CDT.

The workshop therefore served two purposes: it introduced a practical healthcare AI area and showed how research training, local industry and continuing university relationships can reinforce one another.

## Organising structure

The chair described the following role slots:

- Two conference co-chairs
- One financial officer
- One Leeds AI Week chair who also acted as conference webmaster
- Three speaker coordinators
- One marketing lead
- One general volunteer

One co-chair maintained the operational overview while the accountable chair provided overarching support and made the small number of final decisions needed to keep the event moving. Regular Teams calls supported structured planning and a WhatsApp group supported rapid coordination.

Two or three people needed to remain available for last-minute work. Clear responsibility helped, but the chair also recommends recruiting people who can disagree constructively and still support progress. A reusable committee should record who decides when consensus stalls and address repeated unconstructive behaviour early.

## Attendance and timing

The chair reports 135 registrations and estimates that about 74 participants attended, excluding committee members. This remains a first-hand estimate because the workbook contains several partially overlapping attendance records.

Sunday was selected partly because medical students can have hospital placements during the working week. The event also ran the day after Valentine's Day, which the organisers treated as an additional registration and no-show risk.

RSVP confirmation, cancellations and non-response mattered because registrations could exceed room capacity. The chair also emphasises flexibility for low-risk variation, including last-minute arrivals when safe capacity remains.

## Budget, food and promotion

The event had a small budget. A suitable University of Leeds venue was available without a venue charge and sponsorship came through the medical AI CDT. Most spending went on food, with a smaller amount used for one large marketing banner. There was no cash prize money.

Snacks were bought in bulk and pizza was ordered at roughly one pizza for every two people. A small number of leftovers were taken away by attendees and organisers.

Promotion used the AI Society and MedTech Society Instagram accounts, LinkedIn and several university mailing lists.

Exact receipts, sponsorship value, room capacity, food suitability and cost per attendee were not reviewed for this record.

## Venue care and opening housekeeping

Dietary needs were collected through the RSVP process and venue access was considered during selection. No individual attendee's disability or access information should be published.

Opening housekeeping covered photography or consent, fire and evacuation arrangements. A reusable process should also communicate these points before the event, name an access contact and keep any individual requests on a need-to-know basis.

## Programme change

The earlier public agenda included a second workshop. It did not run. The organiser joined the final panel and communicated its main points there.

The chair judged that the day was already information-dense and that another hour would have limited additional value. Some post-lunch attrition was observed, which supported that judgement retrospectively, but it was not formally counted.

The revised schedule was shared on Instagram and announced at the beginning of the day. The supplied schedule poster records the revised timings.

The lesson is not to preserve a session simply because it was advertised. Protect safety, speaker and committee readiness, participant value and core outcomes first. Stay flexible on timing and format when the change is low risk, communicated and recorded.

## Essay and poster activities

The chair recalls approximately eight essay entries and six poster entries. Each activity had one winner and one runner-up.

These entry totals remain approximate until checked against the submission or judging records. The winner and runner-up names should not be published without permission.

## Interpretation

The panel and workshop should be presented as deliberate community design, not as a list of speakers:

- Current CDT researchers demonstrated that there is no single route into a healthcare AI PhD.
- Former CDT researchers demonstrated a route from doctoral training into local employment and continuing collaboration.
- The competition activities gave attendees a reason to contribute, rather than only listen.

Do not imply that the event caused subsequent employment or collaboration. It can demonstrate the existence of networks and relationships, while causal outcomes require separate evidence.
