## Duplicate handling

The conference recap contains seven photographs and the opening keynote post contains four. Three files from the opening keynote post were exact duplicates of files in the recap, verified by SHA-256:

- The second post's healthcare IT landscape photograph is identical to `healthcare-it-landscape.jpg`.
- The second post's wide audience photograph is identical to `audience-wide.jpg`.
- The second post's Mohammad Tasfiq Jawaad photograph is identical to `opening-mohammad-jawaad.jpg`.

Eight distinct photographs were therefore retained from eleven post assets.

LinkedIn displays an `AI-generated content` label within `audience-questions.jpg`. The record does not infer why the label was applied. This file is presented as an illustrative source image and must not be used to establish attendance or another factual claim without separate evidence.

## Excluded organiser-supplied review material

The organiser supplied a Google Drive conference folder containing 141 JPEG
photographs. The folder was inspected read-only on 26 July 2026:

- 134 files at 2048 by 1536 pixels
- 4 files at 1536 by 2048 pixels
- 2 files at 480 by 360 pixels
- 1 file at 360 by 480 pixels
- No byte-identical files within the 141-image folder
- Many near-duplicate sequences from the same talks, panel, audience, poster discussions and committee group

Drive timestamps appear to describe upload or export activity. They are not used as evidence of the event date.

Providing a review link does not by itself establish the photographer's rights
or every identifiable person's consent. The collection URL and selected
committee, poster-discussion and lunch photographs are therefore excluded from
the public site and repository. The supplied schedule image is also excluded;
only its non-personal timetable information is transcribed into the dated
operations record.

A personalised contribution certificate was reviewed but is neither linked nor
copied because it contains a named recipient and named signatories. A reusable
tutorial sample should use a clearly synthetic recipient and role labels.
